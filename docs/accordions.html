Accordions[ Variable Reference ]

Jellyfish accordions are built on the native <details> / <summary> elements. Open/close state, keyboard support and screen-reader semantics are all handled by the browser — the framework JavaScript only adds optional analytics and deep-link conveniences on top.

Style comes from three classes on the native elements: .accordion on the wrapper, .accordion-item on each <details>, .accordion-heading on its <summary>, and .accordion-panel on the content wrapper.

A basic accordion

A shared name on the <details> elements makes the group exclusive — opening one closes the others (the browser's own behaviour, no script). Add open to the item that should start expanded.
Ancient Survivors

Jellyfish have existed for over 500 million years, predating dinosaurs and even trees. Fossil evidence shows they've survived multiple mass extinction events that wiped out countless other species, making them one of Earth's most resilient life forms.

Fishy Imposters

Despite the name, jellyfish aren't fish at all. They're invertebrates called medusae, belonging to the phylum Cnidaria. Scientists prefer the term "jellies" or "sea jellies" to avoid the confusion with actual fish.

Watery Beasts

Jellyfish bodies are composed almost entirely of water, with no brain, heart, or bones. Their simple body structure consists mainly of a gelatinous umbrella-shaped bell and trailing tentacles. Despite lacking a brain, they have a basic nervous system that helps them detect light, odors, and other stimuli.

Exclusive or independent

Give every <details> in a group the same name and only one can be open at a time. Leave name off (or give items different names) and each opens and closes on its own — the equivalent of the old data-allow-multiple. Leave open off every item and the accordion starts fully collapsed — the equivalent of the old data-start-collapsed.
Immortality? Completed it, Mate.

The Turritopsis dohrnii can reverse its aging process and potentially live forever by reverting to its juvenile stage. When faced with injury, starvation, or old age, it transforms back into a polyp and starts its life cycle over again, essentially achieving biological immortality.

That stings!

The box jellyfish has venom potent enough to kill a human in minutes, making it one of the ocean's most dangerous creatures. Their tentacles contain thousands of microscopic stinging cells called nematocysts that inject toxins affecting the heart, nervous system, and skin cells simultaneously.

Animation

Panels open and close with a pure CSS transition — no JavaScript measures or sets a height. The browser's ::details-content pseudo-element animates block-size between 0 and auto, made interpolatable by interpolate-size: allow-keywords scoped to .accordion (not :root — it would change every auto-sized transition in a consuming project). Panel padding sits inside that clipped box, so a closed panel is exactly zero height.

This is progressive enhancement. In a browser without ::details-content / interpolate-size support the panel simply shows and hides with no transition — the accordion is fully functional either way, and no content is ever hidden or clipped. Panels also open instantly when prefers-reduced-motion: reduce is set.

The panel and chevron animate on the framework motion token --jf-transition (var(--jf-duration-base) var(--jf-ease-in-out)). Retune just the accordion by redeclaring it on a scope — .accordion { --jf-transition: 0.4s var(--jf-ease-out); } — or move it framework-wide through $duration-scale. prefers-reduced-motion: reduce zeroes the durations, so there is no per-component setting to turn the animation off.

Colors

If $generate-accordion-modifiers is set to true you can use a color modifier from the $colors map to theme an .accordion or a single .accordion-item.
Giant Tentacles

The lion's mane jellyfish can grow tentacles over 100 feet long, making it longer than a blue whale. Found in cold Arctic waters, the largest recorded specimen had a bell diameter of over 7 feet and tentacles reaching an astounding 120 feet in length.

Glow Up

Many jellyfish species produce their own light through chemical reactions, creating stunning displays in dark waters. This bioluminescence can serve multiple purposes including attracting prey, deterring predators, and even communicating with other jellyfish in the murky depths.

Breathe Easy

They absorb oxygen directly through their skin and have no need for lungs or circulatory systems. Their thin membrane allows gases to diffuse in and out efficiently, while nutrients from digested food spread throughout their body via a simple gastrovascular cavity.

Smack My Fish Up

Groups of jellyfish, called blooms or smacks, can contain hundreds of thousands of individuals and clog fishing nets. These population explosions are becoming more frequent due to warming oceans and overfishing, sometimes shutting down power plants when they block cooling water intake pipes.

Deep linking & in-page search

Because the panels are native <details>, Chromium browsers automatically expand the relevant item when the user runs find-in-page (Ctrl/Cmd + F), follows a #fragment link into it, or lands on a #:~:text= scroll-to-text URL — no attributes, no configuration.

For Firefox and Safari, the enhancement script covers plain fragment links: give a panel (or anything inside it) an id, and a link to #that-id opens its item and scrolls it into view — on click (the script opens the panel first, so the jump lands correctly), on cold load, and on back / forward. Find-in-page on collapsed content stays Chromium-only; there is no cross-browser hook for it on <details>.

Try it on this page

The Watery Beasts panel in the first example above has id="watery-beasts" and starts closed. Test the behaviour:

  • Follow this #watery-beasts link — the panel should open and scroll into view. (It works even from a cold load: open accordions.html#watery-beasts directly, or reload this page with that fragment on the URL.)
  • Close the panel, then use find-in-page (Ctrl/Cmd + F) for a phrase only in that panel, e.g. “gelatinous umbrella” — in Chrome/Edge the panel auto-expands to reveal the match; in Firefox/Safari it won't (no cross-browser hook).
  • In Chrome/Edge, a #:~:text=gelatinous%20umbrella URL also auto-expands the panel on load.

Hooking the enhancement to your own content is just the id — put it on the .accordion-panel, on the <summary>, on the <details>, or on any element inside the panel; the script walks up to the enclosing .accordion-item and opens it.

Tracking

Every open and close pushes to the GTM dataLayer (a no-op unless the page already has GTM installed) and fires a CustomEvent on document — the same pattern as modals. Default-open items (with the open attribute) don't fire on load; only user interaction does.

The title is the <details>'s data-title attribute if present, otherwise its <summary> text.

Migrating from the v4 JavaScript accordion

The v4 accordion — class-based markup (.accordion-item > .accordion-heading + .accordion-panel) upgraded at runtime by accordions.js — is removed in v5. The script, the jfAccordionsInitialised event and the .is-initialised / .is-collapsed / .accordion-button classes it added are gone. Convert the markup:

v4v5
<div class="accordion-item"> <details class="accordion-item"> — add name="…" (shared across the group) to keep the old single-open behaviour
<h3 class="accordion-heading"><span>Title</span></h3> <summary class="accordion-heading">Title</summary> — the browser makes it a focusable, keyboard-operable control; no <button> is injected
<div class="accordion-panel"> Unchanged — still <div class="accordion-panel">, now a child of the <details>
data-allow-multiple Omit the name attribute (independent items are the default for <details>)
data-start-collapsed Omit open from every item
First item open by default Add open to that <details>
document.addEventListener('jfAccordionsInitialised', …) Gone (there is no init step). For open/close hooks, listen for jfAccordionOpened / jfAccordionClosed on document, or the native toggle event on a <details>

The .accordion / .accordion-heading / .accordion-panel class names, the $colors modifiers and every --jf-accordion-* custom property are unchanged.
